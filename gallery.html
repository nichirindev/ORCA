How it works

1 · Prep

The background is knocked out by color distance from the corners, then the subject is cropped square with padding. No solid background? It falls back to photo mode automatically.

2 · Sample

The image shrinks to a ~180-column grid — rows follow the photo's aspect ratio, so the finished art keeps the source's proportions. Each cell becomes one character from the density ramp .`:-=+*cs#%@. Bright cells go sparse, dark cells go dense.

3 · Assemble

One <text> per row — all the same gray — plus a clip wipe and cursor per row, staggered top to bottom. The whole portrait prints in ~6 seconds and holds forever.

Why monochrome? Per-character rainbow color is exactly what makes ASCII portraits look noisy. One ink color (#c9d1d9) over a dark window, a good ramp, and a hard washout floor so busy areas go blank instead of going to mush — that reads as clean type.
